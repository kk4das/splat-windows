Path analysis

One transmitter to one receiver: what signal arrives, and what the terrain in between does to it. Click Analyze a path…, choose the Transmitter and the Receiver (Swap turns the path round), check the radio settings, and click Analyze.

Any terrain tiles the path needs are downloaded first. SPLAT! then runs once for its report and once for each graph; it takes a few seconds. Cancel stops it.

The summary

The box on the left gives SPLAT!'s own numbers, copied word for word from its report:

  • Title: the distance and the direction (azimuth, degrees from true north) from the transmitter to the receiver.
  • Line of sight: whether the straight line between the antennas clears the terrain, or how many points block it.
  • Signal at the receiver: the power a receiving antenna would deliver, in dBm.
  • Field strength: the same signal as field strength, in dBµV/m.
  • Path loss: the total loss between the antennas by the chosen model; free-space loss is what open space alone would cost; terrain loss (attenuation) is the extra the terrain causes.
  • Mode: how the model says the signal gets there (for example line of sight, single or double horizon diffraction).
  • A red line appears if the model reported a problem with the inputs.
  • Clearance: how high the receiver's antenna would have to be to see past the terrain, to clear the first Fresnel zone, and to clear the chosen percentage of it (60% is the usual rule for a path with no extra loss).

The path on the map

The path is drawn between the two stations: solid green when the line of sight is clear, dashed red when terrain blocks it.

The white circles with red rims are the obstructions SPLAT! found: points where the ground rises into the straight line between the antennas. Hover over one to see its height above sea level and its distance from the receiver. The same list is in the Report tab. Blocked doesn't mean no signal: the signal bends over the obstruction (diffraction) at a cost, which SPLAT! includes in the path loss.

The graphs

The tabs under the map are SPLAT!'s graphs, drawn by the app from SPLAT!'s data:

  • Terrain profile: the ground's height along the path.
  • Elevation angle: the angle up (or down) to the terrain along the path, as seen from the receiver, with the line to the transmitter.
  • Height and Fresnel zone: the terrain relative to the straight line between the antennas, with the earth's curvature, the first Fresnel zone and 60% of it. Terrain poking into the Fresnel zone costs signal even when the line of sight is clear.
  • Path loss: the loss along the path, by distance.
  • Report: SPLAT!'s complete report, as it wrote it.

In 3D

3D view… in the summary shows the terrain along the path in 3D, from either antenna or from the side, with the line of sight, the Fresnel zone and SPLAT!'s obstruction points. See The 3D path view.

Afterwards

  • Open in Google Earth Pro (top of the window) shows both stations, the path and its line-of-sight part. See Google Earth.
  • Open the job folder shows everything SPLAT! wrote, and a run-again.cmd that repeats the run from the command line. See Job folders.
  • Back to stations clears the path from the map.
