Terrain

SPLAT! works out what the ground does to a signal from elevation data: one file (.sdf) per 1° × 1° tile. The app finds the tiles each job needs and downloads any it hasn't got, once.

The terrain folder

Tiles are kept in one folder, shown in the path and coverage panels and in Settings. Unless you choose another, it is the app's own folder, %LOCALAPPDATA%\SPLATforWindows\Terrain (local, not in Documents, which OneDrive may sync). If you already have SPLAT! terrain files, choose their folder with Change…; new tiles are added there. Standard and high-definition tiles can share a folder.

Where the terrain comes from

  • Standard terrain (3 arc-second, about 90 m): Jonathan de Ferranti's viewfinderpanoramas.org, in 6° × 4° blocks (a block can be 30 MB, so the first job in a new area takes a minute).
  • High-definition terrain (1 arc-second, about 30 m): the AWS open terrain tiles (Mapzen "skadi").

Each download is unpacked, checked for size and converted with SPLAT!'s own srtm2sdf. A broken or cut-short download is thrown away, never used. Downloads are kept in the folder's raw folder, so nothing is fetched twice. Where a source has no tile, that's open water: the app records it (a .sea file) and SPLAT!'s sea level is then the right answer.

Without terrain for an area SPLAT! quietly treats it as sea level, which gives wrong answers. So the app never runs a job with a tile missing: it downloads it first, or stops and says why.

How much terrain a job can use

SPLAT! holds 64 standard tiles (8° × 8°) or 16 high-definition tiles (4° × 4°) at once. That limits how far apart a path's stations can be, and a coverage map's radius: about 155 miles at mid-latitudes with standard terrain, 66 miles with high-definition. The app checks first and says the limit.

Standard or high-definition?

Standard terrain is right for most VHF and UHF work and is fast. High-definition terrain shows smaller hills and valleys, which matters most for short paths and microwave links, but a coverage map takes several minutes instead of seconds.

Obstacles (user terrain, .udt)

SPLAT!'s user terrain files add buildings, towers or trees on top of the ground. In path analysis (under Ground, climate and model) or a coverage map (under More options), Obstacles > Choose… picks one for the job; Clear removes it. Each line is:

latitude, longitude, height   for example   38.9396, 77.2404, 200

  • Latitude and longitude in decimal degrees, or "degrees minutes seconds".
  • Longitude in degrees WEST, as everywhere in SPLAT!: 77.2404 for 77.2404° W.
  • Height above the ground in feet, or meters if it ends in m (61m).

A file with none of its obstacles inside the job's area is refused: that almost always means the longitudes were written east-negative (−77.24), which SPLAT! would read as 77° east and quietly ignore.
