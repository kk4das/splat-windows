Radio settings

These are the values SPLAT! keeps in a transmitter's .lrp file. The app keeps them with the transmitting station and saves your changes when you click Analyze or Run. Usually only the frequency and the ERP need changing; the rest are SPLAT!'s documented defaults.

The values

SettingWhat it isDefault
Frequency (MHz)The transmitter's frequency. SPLAT! works from 20 MHz to 20 GHz.none
ERP (watts)Effective radiated power: transmitter power, less feed-line loss, plus antenna gain over a dipole. 50 W into a 6 dBd antenna with 2 dB of feed-line loss is about 50 × 100.4 ≈ 126 W ERP.none
PolarizationHorizontal or vertical. FM voice repeaters are vertical.Vertical
GroundThe ground's electrical properties (dielectric constant and conductivity), from SPLAT!'s table: average ground, poor, good, farmland and forest, marshy land, mountain and sand, city and industrial, fresh water, salt water.Average ground (15, 0.005 S/m)
Radio climateOne of seven climates SPLAT! knows. Most of the US east of the Rockies is 5, continental temperate.5 Continental temperate
Situations (%)Location variability. 50% means the answer is what at least half of places of this kind would get.50
Time (%)Time variability. 90% means the signal is at least the answer 90% of the time.90
Atmospheric bending (N-units)How much the atmosphere bends radio waves over the horizon. 301 is the standard atmosphere.301

Load from a .lrp file… fills these in from an existing SPLAT! file.

Path options

Under Ground, climate and model in path analysis:

  • Propagation model: ITWOM 3.0 (SPLAT!'s default) or Longley-Rice ITM, the older model. Longley-Rice sometimes reports "a combination of parameters is out of range": the app shows that warning rather than hiding it.
  • Fresnel clearance (%): how much of the first Fresnel zone must be clear for SPLAT!'s antenna-height advice. 60% is the usual rule.
  • Ground clutter: a height added to all terrain, for trees or buildings (in feet, or meters with metric units).
  • Units in the report: miles and feet, or kilometers and meters.
  • High-definition terrain and Obstacles: see Terrain.

Coverage maps use the same radio settings; their own options are on the Coverage maps page.
