Coverage maps

A coverage map shows how one transmitter's signal reaches the area around it, for a receiver at a given antenna height. Click Coverage map…, choose the transmitter, what to show, the radius and the receiver antenna height, and click Run.

What to show

  • Signal strength (dBµV/m): the field strength a receiver would find, from the transmitter's ERP. The usual choice.
  • Received power (dBm): the power a receiving antenna (a dipole) would deliver. Easiest to compare with a radio's sensitivity.
  • Path loss (dB): the loss between the antennas; the ERP isn't used.
  • Line of sight: where a receiver antenna at that height can see the transmitter's antenna, in green. No radio settings needed.

The radius is in miles (kilometres with metric). SPLAT! can map about 155 miles around a station at mid-latitudes with standard terrain, 66 miles with high-definition terrain; the app says the limit if you ask for more. The receiver antenna height is above the ground everywhere on the map: 6 ft is a car or a handheld, 30 ft a house antenna.

The first map in a new area downloads its terrain first (a minute or so); later maps there reuse it. A standard map takes a few seconds; a high-definition one, several minutes. Cancel stops it.

Reading the colour key

Each colour is a band of signal strength: "at least this much, but less than the next band up". SPLAT!'s key is shown in the panel beside the map, and is in the Google Earth file too. The default signal key:

dBµV/m to dBm for a dipole: dBm ≈ dBµV/m − 20 × log10(frequency in MHz) − 75. That is about −118 at 146 MHz (2 m) and about −128 at 440 MHz (70 cm). The last column is a rule of thumb, not something SPLAT! calculates: buildings, a body-worn handheld or a small antenna each cost roughly 10 to 20 dB, so a handheld indoors needs a band or two more than a car does.

What the map assumes. The situations and time settings (50% and 90% by default) mean the colour is the signal at least half of the places of that colour get, at least 90% of the time. Hills, trees and buildings smaller than the terrain data aren't in it. And the map shows the signal from the transmitter: where you can hear a repeater ("talk-out"). A handheld's 5 W going back to it is much weaker than a repeater's ERP, so at the edges you may hear the repeater but not reach it ("talk-in").

The other maps have their own keys: received power in dBm (0 down to −150 by default), path loss in dB (where less is better), and green for line of sight.

Reading a point: click the map

Click anywhere on a coverage map to see what the map shows there, for example "58 to 68 dBµV/m". The app reads the colour SPLAT! painted at that spot against SPLAT!'s own colour file, so the answer is a band, not SPLAT!'s exact number; the popup says so. With Smooth colour bands it gives an estimate ("about 63 dBµV/m"). On a site marker or a label it says so.

Terrain shading and opacity

Normally SPLAT! paints the areas without a coloured band in grey shades of the terrain (lighter is higher) over the whole area of the terrain tiles, beyond the radius too. Lower the Map opacity slider to see the streets through it.

Or untick Grey terrain shading (under More options): SPLAT! then leaves those areas white, and the app shows them clear, so only the coverage colours sit over the street map, in the app and in Google Earth. SPLAT!'s own map file in the job folder stays as SPLAT! wrote it.

More options

  • Propagation model: ITWOM (SPLAT!'s default) or Longley-Rice.
  • Smooth colour bands: blends the colours between levels instead of hard steps.
  • Kilometres and metres: the radius and receiver height in metric.
  • High-definition terrain: 1 arc-second terrain (about 30 m) instead of 3 arc-second (about 90 m). Finer, slower, smaller maximum radius.
  • Obstacles: a SPLAT! user terrain file; see Terrain.

Ground, climate and the other radio settings are the transmitter's, set in Analyse a path. The transmitter's antenna pattern, if it has one, is used too.

Earlier maps

Every finished map is listed under Earlier maps, newest first. Click one to show it again from its job folder; nothing is run again. Cancelled or failed maps aren't listed.
